The Latency Gap: Why the First Days of a Vacancy Decide Who Wins the Mandate

Companies publish a new role on their own careers page before it is promoted anywhere else, and applications pile up fastest in the first week. The agency that sees the role first talks to the hiring manager before the inbox fills up.

Kryštof Pejša
Founder, TalentRadar • LinkedIn

Where a new role appears first

When a company opens a new role, the first public version of it is almost always the listing on its own careers page or applicant tracking system: Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Workable, Teamio and the like. Publishing there is immediate and costs nothing.

Job boards, aggregators and LinkedIn get the role later, through feeds and crawlers or when someone pays to promote it. How much later varies widely by company and by board, from a few hours to several days. What does not vary is the order: the employer's own page comes first.

That is the page TalentRadar watches. It checks every tracked careers page twice a day, so a new role lands in your morning or midday list at the next scan.

What the data says Source: Ashby, 2023 [1]

Applications pile up in the first week

Ashby analysed about 13 million applications submitted through its ATS between January 2021 and April 2023. The first week of inbound applications was consistently 2.5–3× higher than any following week [1].

The hiring team's attention and the candidate flood both peak right after a role goes live. An agency that reaches the hiring manager in those first days talks to someone who has not yet been buried in CVs and agency emails.

Why the golden window decides who wins the mandate

In engineering, cloud and executive search, hiring managers rarely want ten agencies on the same role. When a critical seat opens, they want a good shortlist quickly, without a pile of unsuitable CVs.

If your agency reaches the Head of Engineering or VP of Sales within the first day or two of the role going live:

  • You are the first external voice they hear. They have not been flooded with recruitment emails yet.
  • You present matched talent immediately. Being one of the first to bring relevant people shows you know the market.
  • You pitch for an exclusive or prioritised engagement. Once the role is widely advertised, procurement and preferred-supplier lists tend to take over and the door to new agencies narrows.

Why roles open past five weeks are warm pitches

SHRM's 2026 benchmarking puts the median time-to-fill at 39 days for non-executive and 45 days for executive roles (US employers, from requisition opened to offer accepted) [2]. A role still open after five or six weeks is at or past that point: the first wave of applicants is long gone and the hiring manager feels it.

TalentRadar takes the posting date from the company’s ATS, or the date it first saw the role when the source gives none, so you can filter for roles open more than 30 or 45 days, find the likely hiring manager and pitch around a specific bottleneck instead of calling cold.

Not every open listing is a real, open job

Greenhouse classifies 18–22% of the jobs posted on its platform in a given quarter as ghost jobs [3]. Add roles that clients quietly filled weeks ago but that still sit in your spreadsheet, and consultants end up sourcing for jobs that no longer exist.

TalentRadar re-checks every careers page twice a day. When a role disappears, the next scan confirms it and the role is archived automatically, so your list of open roles stays honest.
