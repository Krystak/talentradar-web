Why where and how you monitor roles matters

Every agency knows the timing problem: a client opens a key engineering or leadership seat, and by the time a consultant spots it on a job board, the hiring manager already has a crowded inbox and three agencies pitching.

Ashby's analysis of roughly 13 million applications shows that inbound applications in the first week are 2.5–3× higher than in any subsequent week [1]. Meanwhile, in TalentRadar's public demo watching 100 Czech companies as of 3 November 2026 (1,438 open roles total), 45 out of 100 companies are read directly from their own company ATS (24 companies) or custom careers site (21 companies), outside local job boards [4].

How your agency watches those career pages determines whether you reach the hiring manager on day one or after procurement takes over.

Breaking down each approach

1. Manual checks in a spreadsheet or CRM

Most agencies start by splitting a client list across the team and clicking through bookmarks every Monday morning. It costs nothing in software, yet at 50+ accounts it wastes hours of senior consultant time each week—and Tuesday openings sit unseen for six days. Worse, without automated removal checks, recruiters keep sourcing for filled roles (read our breakdown on the cost of dead vacancies).

2. Job board and LinkedIn alerts

Job board alerts are useful for broad market sweeps, but they suffer from two structural blind spots. First is the latency gap between an employer's own ATS and external boards. Second is date masking: when an employer refreshes a paid slot, the board shows a brand-new date even when the role has been stuck open for two months.

3. Generic page-change monitors (Visualping, Distill) or custom scrapers

Website-change monitors alert you when pixels or HTML shift. On modern career sites powered by embedded ATS widgets—such as Greenhouse, Ashby, Workday, SmartRecruiters, or Recruitee—generic monitors frequently trigger false alerts from cookie banners, tracking scripts, or footer edits, and they never build a clean, deduplicated database of roles or import files for Bullhorn and Vincere. Frequently asked questions

Why isn't monitoring job boards like LinkedIn or Jobs.cz enough?

Employers publish new roles on their own career site or ATS (Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby, Workday, Teamio) first because it is free and immediate. Job boards receive postings later through feeds or paid promotion. In TalentRadar's public demo of 100 Czech companies, 45 out of 100 companies are read directly from the company's ATS or custom careers page rather than Jobs.cz or Teamio.

What is the difference between a generic page monitor (like Visualping) and an agency career-page radar?

Generic monitors alert you when raw HTML or pixels change, which breaks on dynamic ATS widgets and does not separate individual job postings from cookie-banner or footer edits. A dedicated tool like TalentRadar parses individual roles, tracks real role age (30+ and 45+ days open), verifies and archives disappeared postings, and exports clean CSV files for Bullhorn, Vincere, or Excel.

How often should a recruitment agency scan client career pages?

Checking every tracked company twice a day—such as 06:00 and 12:00 in your timezone—captures new roles the same day they go live without overloading employer websites.

Do I need to replace my current ATS to monitor client career pages?

No. TalentRadar runs alongside any ATS (Bullhorn, Vincere, Datacruit, Recruitis, Teamio, or spreadsheets) in Radar-only mode with Excel, CSV, and import-ready files. Agencies without an ATS can use the built-in Radar + ATS mode included in every plan.
